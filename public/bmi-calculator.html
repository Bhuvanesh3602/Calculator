    BMI Introduction

    Use the "Metric Units" tab for the International System of Units, or the "Other Units" tab to convert units to US or metric.

    BMI is a measure of a person's leanness or corpulence based on height and weight. It is widely used as a general indicator of whether a person has a healthy body weight for their height. The value is used to classify a person as underweight, normal weight, overweight or obese, and the ranges vary with factors such as region and age. They are sometimes divided into subcategories such as severely underweight or very severely obese. Being overweight or underweight can have significant health effects, so although BMI is an imperfect measure, it is a useful signal of whether further testing or action is needed.

    Risks Associated with Being Overweight

    According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), being overweight increases the risk of:

    • High blood pressure
    • Higher LDL ("bad") cholesterol, lower HDL ("good") cholesterol and high triglycerides
    • Type 2 diabetes
    • Coronary heart disease
    • Stroke
    • Gallbladder disease
    • Osteoarthritis, a joint disease caused by breakdown of joint cartilage
    • Sleep apnea and breathing problems
    • Certain cancers (endometrial, breast, colon, kidney, gallbladder, liver)
    • Lower quality of life
    • Mental health conditions such as clinical depression and anxiety
    • Body pain and difficulty with physical functions
    • Generally, a higher risk of death compared with a healthy BMI

    Generally, a person should try to keep their BMI below 25 kg/m2, but should consult a doctor to decide whether any lifestyle changes are needed.

    Risks Associated with Being Underweight

    • Malnutrition, vitamin deficiencies and anemia (a reduced ability of the blood to carry oxygen)
    • Osteoporosis, a disease that weakens bones and raises the risk of fractures
    • Decreased immune function
    • Growth and development problems, particularly in children and teenagers
    • Possible reproductive issues for women: hormonal imbalances can disrupt the menstrual cycle, and underweight women have a higher chance of miscarriage in the first trimester
    • Potential complications from surgery
    • Generally, a higher risk of death compared with a healthy BMI

    Being underweight can sometimes signal an underlying condition such as anorexia nervosa. Consult a doctor if you think you or someone you know is underweight, particularly if the reason is not obvious.

    Limitations of BMI

    BMI is an estimate that cannot take body composition into account. Because body types and the distribution of muscle, bone and fat vary widely, BMI should be considered alongside other measurements rather than used as the only way to judge healthy body weight.

    In Adults

    BMI measures excess body weight rather than excess body fat, and it is influenced by age, sex, ethnicity, muscle mass, body fat and activity level. For example, an inactive older person at a "healthy" weight may carry a lot of excess body fat, while a younger person with more muscle at the same BMI may be healthy. Bodybuilders and other athletes can be classed as overweight simply because muscle is heavier than fat. According to the CDC:

    • Older adults tend to have more body fat than younger adults with the same BMI.
    • Women tend to have more body fat than men at an equivalent BMI.
    • Muscular individuals and highly trained athletes may have higher BMIs because of large muscle mass.

    In Children and Adolescents

    The same limits apply to children and adolescents, and height and sexual maturation can also influence BMI and body fat. BMI is a better indicator of excess body fat for obese children than for overweight children, whose BMI may reflect more fat or more fat-free mass (water, organs, muscle and everything except fat). In thin children, differences in BMI can also come from fat-free mass.

    That said, BMI is fairly indicative of body fat for about 90-95% of the population and can be used with other measures to help judge a healthy body weight.

    BMI Formula

    The equations below use a 5'10", 160-pound person as an example.

    US Customary Units

    BMI = 703 × mass (lbs) ÷ height² (in) = 703 × 160 ÷ 70² = 23.0

    Metric (SI) Units

    BMI = mass (kg) ÷ height² (m) = 72.57 ÷ 1.778² = 23.0

    BMI Prime

    BMI Prime is the ratio of a person's BMI to the upper limit of the "normal" range used by bodies such as the WHO and CDC. That upper limit, BMIupper, is 25 kg/m2, though it may differ in some countries, such as in Asia.

    BMI Prime = BMI ÷ 25

    BMI Prime is dimensionless. Below 0.74 is underweight, 0.74 to 1 is normal, above 1 is overweight and above 1.2 is obese.

    Ponderal Index

    The Ponderal Index (PI) also measures leanness or corpulence from height and weight, but cubes the height instead of squaring it. BMI is useful for large populations but unreliable for individuals. The PI has similar limits but is more reliable for very tall or very short people, for whom BMI tends to give unusually high or low body fat estimates.

    US Customary Units

    PI = height (in) ÷ ∛mass (lbs) = 70 ÷ ∛160 = 12.9

    Metric (SI) Units

    PI = mass (kg) ÷ height³ (m) = 72.57 ÷ 1.778³ = 12.9

    BMI Calculator FAQ

    What is a healthy BMI?

    For adults, the WHO defines a normal BMI as 18.5 to 25 kg/m². Below 18.5 is underweight, 25 to 30 is overweight, and 30 or above is obese.

    How do I calculate BMI?

    In metric units, BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m). In US units, BMI = 703 × weight (lbs) ÷ height² (in). A 5'10" (70 in), 160 lb person has a BMI of 703 × 160 ÷ 70² = 23.0.

    How is BMI interpreted for children and teens?

    For ages 2 to 20 the CDC uses BMI-for-age percentiles instead of fixed cut-offs: underweight is below the 5th percentile, healthy weight is the 5th to below the 85th, at risk of overweight is the 85th to below the 95th, and overweight is the 95th or higher.

    Is BMI accurate?

    BMI is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. It measures excess weight rather than excess body fat, so it can misclassify muscular people and athletes, older adults, and people of different ethnicities. Use it together with other measures and talk to a doctor.

    What is BMI Prime?

    BMI Prime is your BMI divided by 25, the upper limit of the normal range. A value between 0.74 and 1 is normal, above 1 is overweight, and above 1.2 is obese.

    What is the Ponderal Index?

    The Ponderal Index is weight divided by height cubed (kg/m³). It is similar to BMI but is considered more reliable for very tall or very short people.

    Is the BMI the same for men and women?

    The adult BMI formula and WHO categories are the same for men and women, but women tend to have more body fat than men at the same BMI.

    What BMI is considered underweight?

    An adult BMI below 18.5 is classed as underweight: mild thinness 17 to 18.5, moderate thinness 16 to 17, and severe thinness below 16.
