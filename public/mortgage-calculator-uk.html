How Your Monthly Mortgage Payment Is Calculated

For a standard repayment mortgage the monthly payment is:

M = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

where P is the loan amount (home price minus deposit), r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12) and n is the number of monthly payments (years × 12).

Example

A £500,000 home with a 25% deposit gives a £375,000 loan. At 5% over 25 years (300 payments), r = 0.05 ÷ 12 and the monthly repayment is about £2,192.21. Over the full term you would pay about £657,664 in total, of which £282,664 is interest.

Repayment vs Interest-Only Mortgage

With a repayment mortgage each payment covers the interest plus part of the loan, so the debt is fully cleared at the end of the term. With an interest-only mortgage you pay just the interest, so the monthly cost is lower, but you still owe the whole loan at the end and need a separate plan to repay it.

Interest-only payment = loan × annual rate ÷ 12 = £375,000 × 5% ÷ 12 = £1,562.50

Applying for a Mortgage in the UK

It helps potential borrowers to arrive prepared with the information a lender may ask for:

  1. addresses for the last three years, with no gaps
  2. income information for the last three months, as well as three years of income records
  3. bank statements for the past three months, though more can help
  4. documentation of loans or credit cards

A good second step is to check your credit score and raise any questionable negative marks with the lender. With good credit it is possible to borrow with a relatively small deposit: many mortgage products require a deposit of 5% or less of the property value, although this changes with market conditions.

Tracker Mortgage

A tracker mortgage follows the movements of another rate, most commonly the Bank of England base rate. Most UK banks favour variable-rate mortgages in one form or another, so there is a wider variety of them in the UK. Some mortgages start as trackers and after a few years become standard variable-rate mortgages.

Introductory tracker rates can be among the lowest available, but like all variable rates they can go up as well as down. Most introductory trackers will also carry an early repayment charge if you remortgage or repay during the introductory period, and lifetime trackers usually carry early repayment charges for a period after being taken out.

Flexible Mortgage

A flexible mortgage lets you overpay, underpay or take a payment holiday from time to time. Overpayments can pay the loan down faster, either in lump sums or as part of regular payments. Underpayments allow lower payments occasionally, and payment holidays can pause payments for a short time, sometimes up to six months. These features usually come with special charges and conditions, so shop around and compare the costs.

Stamp Duty (England and Northern Ireland)

Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) is charged on the purchase price of a property in England and Northern Ireland, in bands (you pay each rate only on the slice of the price within that band). The standard residential rates in force since 1 April 2025 are:

Part of the priceStandard rate
Up to £125,0000%
£125,001 to £250,0002%
£250,001 to £925,0005%
£925,001 to £1,500,00010%
Over £1,500,00012%

First-time buyers pay 0% on the first £300,000 and 5% on the portion from £300,001 to £500,000. There is no first-time buyer relief if the price is over £500,000, and the standard rates apply instead.

Example: on a £500,000 home, a standard buyer pays 0% on the first £125,000, 2% on the next £125,000 (£2,500) and 5% on the remaining £250,000 (£12,500), a total of £15,000. An eligible first-time buyer pays 5% on £200,000 (£10,000), a total of £10,000.

Buyers of additional properties, such as second homes and buy-to-lets, pay a 5% surcharge on top of these rates, and non-UK residents pay a further 2% surcharge. Other special rules apply, for example to shared ownership, companies, purchasing six or more properties in one transaction, and linked transactions.

Scotland and Wales: Scotland charges Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT) and Wales charges Land Transaction Tax (LTT), each with different rates and thresholds. Stamp duty can be complex and rates change, so check the current figures on GOV.UK and speak to a professional.

UK Mortgage Calculator FAQ

How do I calculate my monthly mortgage payment?

Use M = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12) and n is the number of monthly payments. For a £375,000 loan at 5% over 25 years (300 payments), the repayment is about £2,192 per month.

What is the difference between repayment and interest-only?

With a repayment mortgage each payment covers interest and some of the loan, so the debt is cleared by the end of the term. With interest-only you pay just the interest, so the full loan is still owed at the end and you need a separate plan to repay it. On £375,000 at 5%, interest-only is £1,562.50 a month.

How much deposit do I need for a mortgage in the UK?

Many lenders offer mortgages with a deposit of 5% of the property price, though larger deposits usually unlock lower interest rates. Availability changes with market conditions.

How much stamp duty will I pay?

In England and Northern Ireland, Stamp Duty Land Tax is charged on bands of the price: 0% up to £125,000, 2% from £125,001 to £250,000, 5% from £250,001 to £925,000, 10% from £925,001 to £1.5 million and 12% above that. First-time buyers pay 0% up to £300,000 and 5% from £300,001 to £500,000 and get no relief above £500,000. Scotland (LBTT) and Wales (LTT) use different rules. Always confirm current rates on GOV.UK.

What is a tracker mortgage?

A tracker mortgage follows another rate, most commonly the Bank of England base rate, plus a set margin. Your payments can go up or down when that rate moves, and there may be early repayment charges.

What is a flexible mortgage?

A flexible mortgage lets you overpay, underpay or take a payment holiday. These features often come with conditions or charges, so compare the total cost.

What documents do I need to apply for a mortgage?

Lenders usually ask for your addresses for the last three years with no gaps, proof of income (typically recent payslips and several years of records), around three months of bank statements, and details of any loans or credit cards.

Can I overpay my mortgage?

Often yes, but many fixed-rate deals limit how much you can overpay each year without an early repayment charge. Check your offer or ask your lender before overpaying.
