How to Use the Ratio Calculator

  1. Solve for a missing value: enter any three of A, B, C, and D in A:B = C:D, leave one blank, and click Calculate. The result uses D = B × C ÷ A (or the matching form for the blank you left).
  2. Scale a ratio: enter the two terms, choose Enlarge or Shrink, enter the number of times, and click Calculate. Shrinking 250:280 by 2.5 times gives 100:112.
  3. Simplify by hand: divide both terms by their greatest common factor. For example, 12:18 becomes 2:3 after dividing by 6.

What is Ratio?

A ratio is a quantitative relationship between two numbers that describe how many times one value can contain another. Applications of ratios are fairly ubiquitous, and the concept of ratios is quite intuitive. This could likely be demonstrated by giving a child half as many cookies as his sister. While the child may not be able to voice the injustice using ratios, the raucous protestations that would most likely ensue should make it immediately obvious that he is well aware he has received 1:2 as many cookies as his sister, conceptually, if not mathematically.

As shown above, ratios are often expressed as two numbers separated by a colon. They can also be written as "1 to 2" or as a fraction ½. The ratio represents the number that needs to be multiplied by the denominator in order to yield the numerator. In this case, ½. This is clearer if the first number is larger than the second, i.e. with the ratio 2:1, 2 can contain 1, 2 times. It is also possible to have ratios that have more than two terms.

Ratios are common in many daily applications including: aspect ratios for screens, describing maps and models as a scaled-down version of their actual size, in baking and cooking, when discussing the odds of something occurring, or to describe rates, such as in finance. If, for example, a person wanted to make 5 cakes, each of which required a 1:2:3 ratio of butter:sugar:flour, and wanted to determine the total amount of butter, sugar, and flour that is necessary, it would be simple to compute given the ratio. Increasing the ratio by five times yields a 5:10:15 ratio, and this can be multiplied by whatever the actual amount of sugar, flour, and butter are used in the actual cake recipe.

Typical Aspect Ratios and Sizes of Screens and Videos

The aspect ratio is the ratio of a geometric shape's sizes in different dimensions. In the case of a rectangle, the aspect ratio is that of its width to its height. Although aspect ratios are widely used in applications such as tire sizing, paper sizing, and standard photographic print sizes, some of the most frequent uses of aspect ratios involve computer screen dimensions, mobile phone screens, and video sizes. Ratio Calculator FAQs

Quick answers to the questions people ask most about ratios.

What is a ratio?

A ratio is a quantitative relationship between two numbers that shows how many times one value contains another. It can be written with a colon (1:2), with the word "to" (1 to 2), or as a fraction (1/2).

How do I calculate a ratio?

Put the two quantities in the same order and the same units, then write them as A:B. To compare 15 apples with 5 oranges, the ratio is 15:5, which simplifies to 3:1. Use the calculator above to solve for a missing value.

How do I find the missing value in a ratio A:B = C:D?

Cross multiply. The fourth value is D = B × C ÷ A. With the default example, 3:4 = 600:D gives D = 4 × 600 ÷ 3 = 800. The same pattern solves for any other missing value: A = B × C ÷ D, B = A × D ÷ C, and C = A × D ÷ B.

What is the difference between a ratio and a proportion?

A ratio compares two quantities, such as 3:4. A proportion is a statement that two ratios are equal, such as 3:4 = 600:800. The calculator solves proportions by finding the missing term.

How do I simplify a ratio?

Divide every term by their greatest common factor. For 12:18, the greatest common factor is 6, so the simplified ratio is 2:3. If you need to work with the underlying numbers as fractions, try the Fraction Calculator.

How do I scale a ratio up or down?

Multiply or divide both terms by the same number. Shrinking 250:280 by 2.5 times gives 100:112, which is what the ratio scaling calculator shows by default. Enlarging 3:4 by 5 times gives 15:20.

How do I write a ratio?

Common forms are 1:2, "1 to 2", and the fraction 1/2. All three say the same thing. A ratio can also have more than two terms, such as 1:2:3.

Can a ratio have more than two numbers?

Yes. A recipe might call for butter, sugar, and flour in a 1:2:3 ratio. To make five batches, multiply every term by 5 to get 5:10:15, then multiply by the actual unit amount.

How do I use ratios in cooking and recipes?

Keep the ingredient ratio fixed and change the batch size. If one cake uses butter, sugar, and flour in a 1:2:3 ratio, five cakes use five times each amount. Scaling the ratio preserves the taste and texture.

How do I convert a ratio to a fraction or percentage?

For a part-to-part ratio A:B, the first part's share of the whole is A ÷ (A + B). The ratio 1:3 means 1 out of 4 parts, which is 1/4 or 25%, and the other part is 75%. The Percentage Calculator can help with the percentage step.

What is an aspect ratio?

An aspect ratio is the ratio of a shape's width to its height. It is used for screens, video, photos, paper sizes, and tires. A 1920 × 1080 display has an aspect ratio of 16:9.

What is a 16:9 aspect ratio?

It means the width is 16 units for every 9 units of height. It is the standard for HD and 4K video, including 1280 × 720 (720p), 1920 × 1080 (1080p), and 3840 × 2160 (4K UHD). See the table above for more sizes.

How do I find the height from the width using an aspect ratio?

Multiply the width by the height part of the ratio, then divide by the width part. For a 16:9 video that is 1280 pixels wide, the height is 1280 × 9 ÷ 16 = 720 pixels. Enter A = 16, B = 9, and C = 1280 in the calculator to get D.

What is the difference between a ratio and a fraction?

A ratio such as 3:1 compares one part to another part. A fraction such as 3/4 compares a part to the whole. The ratio 3:1 describes the same split as the fractions 3/4 and 1/4.

Yes. It is free to use and needs no sign-up. Results are calculated from the numbers you enter, so check that your values use the same units and order.
