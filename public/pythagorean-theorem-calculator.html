What Is the Pythagorean Theorem?

The Pythagorean Theorem, also known as Pythagoras' theorem, is a fundamental relation between the three sides of a right triangle. Given a right triangle, which is a triangle in which one of the angles is 90°, the Pythagorean theorem states that the area of the square formed by the longest side of the right triangle (the hypotenuse) is equal to the sum of the area of the squares formed by the other two sides of the right triangle:

In other words, given that the longest side c = the hypotenuse, and a and b = the other sides of the triangle:

a2 + b2 = c2

This is known as the Pythagorean equation, named after the ancient Greek thinker Pythagoras. This relationship is useful because if two sides of a right triangle are known, the Pythagorean theorem can be used to determine the length of the third side. Referencing the above diagram, if

a = 3 and b = 4

the length of c can be determined as:

c = √a2 + b2 = √32+42 = √25 = 5

It follows that the length of a and b can also be determined if the lengths of the other two sides are known using the following relationships:

a = √c2 - b2

b = √c2 - a2

The law of cosines is a generalization of the Pythagorean theorem that can be used to determine the length of any side of a triangle if the lengths and angles of the other two sides of the triangle are known. If the angle between the other sides is a right angle, the law of cosines reduces to the Pythagorean equation.

There are a multitude of proofs for the Pythagorean theorem, possibly even the greatest number of any mathematical theorem.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the Pythagorean theorem?

The Pythagorean theorem states that in a right triangle, the square of the hypotenuse (the longest side) equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides. It is written as a2 + b2 = c2, where c is the hypotenuse.

How do I use this Pythagorean theorem calculator?

If a side is a square root, such as 2√5, enter the 2 in the first box and the 5 in the box under the radical sign. For example, the default values (a = 2 and c = 2√5) give b = 4.

How do I find the hypotenuse of a right triangle?

Square both legs, add them together, and take the square root: c = √(a2 + b2). For example, if a = 3 and b = 4, then c = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5.

How do I find a missing leg of a right triangle?

Subtract the square of the known leg from the square of the hypotenuse, then take the square root. The formulas are a = √(c2 – b2) and b = √(c2 – a2).

Which side is the hypotenuse?

The hypotenuse is the longest side of a right triangle and is always the side opposite the 90° angle. It is labeled c in the Pythagorean equation.

Does the Pythagorean theorem work for all triangles?

No. It only works for right triangles, which have one 90° angle. For other triangles, the law of cosines can be used. It is a generalization of the Pythagorean theorem and reduces to a2 + b2 = c2 when the angle between sides a and b is 90°.

What are Pythagorean triples?

Pythagorean triples are sets of three positive whole numbers that satisfy a2 + b2 = c2. Common examples are 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17 and 7-24-25. Multiples of a triple, such as 6-8-10, also work.

How can I check whether a triangle is a right triangle?

Square the three side lengths. If the sum of the two smaller squares equals the square of the longest side, the triangle is a right triangle. This works because the converse of the Pythagorean theorem is also true.

Can the hypotenuse be shorter than another side?

No. Since c2 = a2 + b2, the hypotenuse is always longer than either leg. If you get a hypotenuse shorter than a leg, one of the values was entered incorrectly or the triangle is not a right triangle.

How do I find the area and perimeter of a right triangle after finding the missing side?

Once all three sides are known, the area is (a × b) / 2, using the two legs as base and height, and the perimeter is a + b + c. The right triangle calculator can work these out for you.

How do I find the angles of a right triangle?

One angle is 90°. The other two can be found with trigonometry: the angle opposite side a is arctan(a / b), and the angle opposite side b is arctan(b / a). The two acute angles always add up to 90°.

Where is the Pythagorean theorem used in real life?

It is used in construction to make sure corners are square, in navigation and surveying to find straight-line distances, to calculate the diagonal of a screen or room, to find the length of a ladder needed to reach a height, and in the distance formula in coordinate geometry.

Do the sides need to use the same units?

Yes. All sides must be in the same unit, such as centimeters, meters, inches or feet. The calculated side will then be in that same unit.

Who discovered the Pythagorean theorem?

The theorem is named after the ancient Greek thinker Pythagoras. The relationship was also known in other ancient cultures. There are a great many proofs of it, possibly more than for any other mathematical theorem, and two of the simplest are shown above.
