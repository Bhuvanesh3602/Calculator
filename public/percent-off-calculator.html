Additional discount

A percent off of a product or service is a common discount format. A percent off of a product means that the price of the product is reduced by that percent. For example, given a product that costs $279, 20% off of that product would mean subtracting 20% of the original price from the original price. For example:

20% of $279 = 0.20 × 279 = $55.80

$279 - $55.80 = $223.20

You would therefore be saving $55.80 on the purchase for a final price of $223.20.

For this calculator, a "stackable additional discount" means getting a further percent off of a product after a discount is applied. Using the same example, assume that the 20% discount is a discount applied by the store to the product. If you have a coupon for another 15% off, the 15% off would then be applied to the discounted price of $223.20. It is not a total of 35% off of the original price. It is less:

15% of $223.20 = $33.48

$223.20 - $33.48 = $189.72

Thus, with a 20% discount off of $279, and an additional 15% off of that discounted price, you would end up saving a total of:

$55.80 + $33.48 = $89.28

This equates to a 32% discount, rather than a 35% discount, and this calculation is how the calculator is intended to be used. As an example, to more efficiently compute the discount described above:

Final price = (0.80 × 279) × 0.85 = $189.72

This is because 80% of the original price is the same as subtracting 20% of the original price from the original price. The same is true for 85% and 15% case applied to the discounted price.

Percent Off Formulas

Final price: original price × (1 − percent off ÷ 100)

Amount saved: original price × percent off ÷ 100

Original price: final price ÷ (1 − percent off ÷ 100)

Percent off: (original price − final price) ÷ original price × 100

Two stacked discounts: original price × (1 − first discount) × (1 − second discount)

For example, 15% off $39.99 saves about $6.00, giving a final price of about $33.99.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate the price after a percent off?
Multiply the original price by (1 - percent off / 100). For example, 20% off $279 is 279 x 0.80 = $223.20, so you save $55.80.

How do I calculate how much I save?
Multiply the original price by the percent off divided by 100. For example, 15% off $39.99 saves 39.99 x 0.15 = about $6.00, for a final price of about $33.99.

How do I find the original price from the sale price?
Divide the sale price by (1 - percent off / 100). For example, if an item costs $85 after 15% off, the original price was 85 / 0.85 = $100.

How do I find the percent off from two prices?
Subtract the final price from the original price, divide by the original price and multiply by 100. For example, $80 down from $100 is (100 - 80) / 100 x 100 = 20% off.

Do two discounts add together?
No. A second discount applies to the already reduced price, so 20% off followed by 15% off is not 35% off. On $279 it gives a final price of $189.72, which is a 32% total discount.

How do I calculate two stacked discounts quickly?
Multiply the original price by (1 - first discount) and then by (1 - second discount). For 20% and 15% off $279: 279 x 0.80 x 0.85 = $189.72.

Does the order of stacked discounts matter?
No. When both discounts are percentages, the final price is the same in either order because multiplication gives the same result. 15% then 20% off also gives $189.72 on a $279 item.
