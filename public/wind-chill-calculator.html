What is wind chill and how does it work?

Your body keeps a thin layer of warm air next to your skin. Wind strips that layer away, so heat leaves faster and the air feels colder than the thermometer says. The wind chill temperature is the calm-air temperature that would cool exposed skin at the same rate. It describes the effect on people, not on objects: a car engine or a water pipe never drops below the actual air temperature.

How do I stay safe in the cold?

  • Wear layers: a wicking base, an insulating middle, and a windproof shell.
  • Cover ears, nose, hands, and feet first, since they freeze first.
  • Keep clothing dry and avoid tight boots or gloves that restrict blood flow.
  • Watch for numbness, white or waxy skin, and uncontrolled shivering, then get inside.
  • Do not rub frozen skin. Warm it gradually and seek medical care for severe cases.

Is wind chill the same as the actual temperature?

No. The air temperature does not change. Wind chill only describes how fast bare skin loses heat.

Does wind chill affect cars, pipes, or pets' water bowls?

No. Objects never cool below the air temperature, though wind helps them reach it faster. Animals and people feel the effect.
