How to Use the P-value Calculator

  1. Enter one value – type a Z-score, or any one of the p-value fields (left tail, right tail, from center, between, or two tails).

What Is a P-value?

A p-value (probability value) is a value used in statistical hypothesis testing that is intended to determine whether the obtained results are significant. In statistical hypothesis testing, the null hypothesis is a type of hypothesis that states a default position, such as there is no association among groups or relationship between two observations. Assuming that the given null hypothesis is correct, a p-value is the probability of obtaining test results in an experiment that are at least as extreme as the observed results. In other words, determining a p-value helps you determine how likely it is that the observed results actually differ from the null hypothesis.

The smaller the p-value, the higher the significance, and the more evidence there is that the null hypothesis should be rejected for an alternative hypothesis. Typically, a p-value of ≤ 0.05 is accepted as significant and the null hypothesis is rejected, while a p-value > 0.05 indicates that there is not enough evidence against the null hypothesis to reject it.

Given that the data being studied follows a normal distribution, a Z-score table can be used to determine p-values, as in this calculator.

Z-score Formula

z = (x – μ) / σ

Here x is the observed value, μ is the mean and σ is the standard deviation. When testing a sample mean against a known population mean, use z = (x̄ – μ) / (σ / √n), where n is the sample size.

P-value Calculator FAQ: Frequently Asked Questions

What is a p-value?

A p-value is the probability of obtaining results at least as extreme as the observed results, assuming the null hypothesis is true. A small p-value means the data would be unlikely if the null hypothesis were correct, which counts as evidence against it.

How do I find the p-value from a Z-score?

Enter the Z-score in the calculator and click Calculate. The p-value is the area under the standard normal curve in the tail you care about. For example, Z = 2 gives a right-tail p-value of about 0.0228 and a two-tailed p-value of about 0.0455.

How do I calculate a Z-score?

A Z-score is z = (x − mean) / standard deviation. When testing a sample mean against a known population mean, use z = (sample mean − population mean) / (standard deviation / √n).

Should I use the left-tail, right-tail or two-tailed p-value?

Use the left-tail p-value when your alternative hypothesis says the true value is less than the null value, the right-tail p-value when it says greater than, and the two-tailed p-value when it says different from (either direction).

What is the difference between a one-tailed and a two-tailed p-value?

A one-tailed p-value looks at extreme results in only one direction, while a two-tailed p-value counts extreme results in both directions. For a symmetric distribution such as the normal distribution, the two-tailed p-value is twice the one-tailed p-value.

What Z-score corresponds to a p-value of 0.05?

For a two-tailed test at 0.05, the critical Z-scores are about ±1.96. For a one-tailed test at 0.05, the critical Z-score is about 1.645 (right tail) or −1.645 (left tail).

Is a p-value less than 0.05 significant?

By the most common convention, a p-value of 0.05 or less is called statistically significant and the null hypothesis is rejected. The 0.05 cutoff is a convention, not a law, and some fields use stricter levels such as 0.01 or 0.001.

Does the p-value tell me the probability that the null hypothesis is true?

No. The p-value is calculated assuming the null hypothesis is true. It does not give the probability that the null hypothesis is true, and it does not measure the size or importance of an effect.

Can a p-value be 0?

In theory the p-value for a normal distribution is never exactly 0, because the tails of the curve extend infinitely. Software may display 0 or a very small number when the Z-score is so large that the result is below the displayed precision.

Can I use this calculator for a t-test or chi-square test?

This calculator is for the normal (Z) distribution only. A t-test uses the t-distribution, which depends on degrees of freedom, and a chi-square test uses the chi-square distribution. With a large sample size the t-distribution is close to the normal distribution, so the Z result is a reasonable approximation.

What is the difference between the "from center" and "between" p-values?

The "from center" value is the area between 0 and Z (one half of the middle region), while the "between" value is the area between −Z and Z. The "between" value is twice the "from center" value.
