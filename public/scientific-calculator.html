How to Use the Scientific Calculator

Click the buttons or type on your keyboard to enter a calculation. Your entry appears on the upper line of the display, and the result appears on the lower line. Press = to calculate, Back to delete the last entry, and AC to clear everything. Previous calculations are listed in the history area under the keypad.

Before using trigonometric functions, choose Deg (degrees) or Rad (radians). The same input gives a different result in each mode. For example, sin(90) = 1 in degree mode, while sin(π/2) = 1 in radian mode.

Order of Operations

By mathematical convention, expressions are evaluated in this order: parentheses, exponents, multiplication and division (left to right), then addition and subtraction (left to right). Use the ( and ) buttons whenever you want to make the order explicit.

Common Uses of a Scientific Calculator

A scientific calculator goes beyond basic arithmetic. It is commonly used for algebra, geometry and trigonometry homework, physics and chemistry problems, engineering calculations, and any task that needs logarithms, exponents, roots or very large and small numbers. For numbers in scientific notation, see the Scientific Notation Calculator.

Scientific Calculator FAQs

How do I use this online scientific calculator?

Click the on-screen buttons or type on your keyboard, just as you would on a physical calculator. Your entry appears on the upper line of the display and the result on the lower line. Press = to get the answer and AC to clear everything.

How do I switch between degrees and radians?

Use the Deg and Rad options next to the sin, cos and tan buttons. In Deg mode, sin(90) = 1. In Rad mode, sin(π/2) = 1. Check the mode before calculating trigonometric functions, because the same input gives different results in each mode.

How do I calculate sin, cos, tan and their inverses?

Press the function button (sin, cos or tan) and enter the angle. In degree mode, sin(30) = 0.5, cos(60) = 0.5 and tan(45) = 1. For inverse functions use sin-1, cos-1 and tan-1: in degree mode, sin-1(0.5) = 30.

How do I calculate powers and roots?

Use x2 to square, x3 to cube, xy for any power, √x for the square root, 3√x for the cube root and y√x for any root. For example, 210 = 1,024 and the cube root of 27 is 3. The ex and 10x buttons raise e or 10 to a power.

What is the difference between ln and log?

ln is the natural logarithm, with base e (about 2.71828). log is the common logarithm, with base 10. For example, ln(e) = 1 and log(1000) = 3.

How do I calculate a factorial?

Enter a whole number and press n!. The factorial multiplies all whole numbers from 1 up to that number, so 5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120.

How do I enter numbers in scientific notation?

Use the EXP button to enter a power of 10. For example, 3 EXP 8 means 3 × 108. To convert numbers to scientific notation or do arithmetic with it, use the Scientific Notation Calculator on this site.

How do the memory buttons (M+, M-, MR) and Ans work?

M+ adds the current value to memory, M- subtracts it from memory, and MR recalls the stored value. Ans inserts the result of your previous calculation so you can use it in the next one.

How do I control the order of operations?

Use the ( and ) buttons to group parts of an expression. By mathematical convention, parentheses are evaluated first, then exponents, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. When in doubt, add parentheses to make your intent explicit.

What are π and e on a scientific calculator?

π (pi) is the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter, about 3.14159. e is Euler's number, the base of natural logarithms, about 2.71828. Press the π or e button to insert them into a calculation.

How do I find the reciprocal or a percentage?

Press 1/x to get the reciprocal of a number, so 1/x of 4 is 0.25. Use the % button for percentages. For more involved percentage problems, such as percentage change, use the Percentage Calculator on this site.

Can I use this calculator on my phone or tablet?

Yes. It runs in any modern web browser on phones, tablets and computers. Tap the buttons on a touch screen, or type with a keyboard on a computer. No download or sign-up is needed.
