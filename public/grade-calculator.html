How to Calculate a Weighted Grade

A weighted grade counts each assignment by how much it is worth. Multiply every grade by its weight, add the results, and divide by the total weight.

Weighted grade = (g₁×w₁ + g₂×w₂ + … + gₙ×wₙ) ÷ (w₁ + w₂ + … + wₙ)

Example

Homework 90% (weight 5), Project 85% (weight 20), Midterm 88% (weight 20):

(90×5 + 85×20 + 88×20) ÷ (5 + 20 + 20) = 3910 ÷ 45 = 86.9% (B+)

How to Calculate the Grade Needed on a Final Exam

Needed score = (Goal − Current × (1 − F)) ÷ F    (F = final weight as a decimal)

With a current grade of 88%, a goal of 85%, and a final worth 40%: (85 − 88×0.6) ÷ 0.4 = 80.5%.

Grade Calculator FAQ

How do I calculate my weighted grade?

Multiply each grade by its weight (as a decimal), add the results, then divide by the total of the weights used. Formula: Weighted grade = (g1×w1 + g2×w2 + … + gn×wn) ÷ (w1 + w2 + … + wn).

How do I calculate the grade I need on my final exam?

Use: Needed score = (Goal − Current × (1 − F)) ÷ F, where F is the final exam weight as a decimal. Example: current grade 88%, goal 85%, final worth 40% → (85 − 88×0.6) ÷ 0.4 = 80.5%.

What is a weighted average grade?

A weighted average gives each assignment a different importance. A final exam worth 40% affects your course grade far more than a homework worth 5%.

Can I use letter grades in this calculator?

Yes. Open Settings, choose Letters, and select A+ through F for each assignment. Letters are converted using the standard 4.3 GPA scale shown on this page.

What percentage is an A, B, or C?

On the common US scale: A is 93–96%, A- is 90–92%, B+ is 87–89%, B is 83–86%, C is 73–76%, D is 63–66%, and F is below 60%. Your school may use a different scale.

What if my weights do not add up to 100%?

The calculator divides by the total of the weights you entered, so the result is still your average over the work completed so far. Use the Remaining Tasks weight to plan for unfinished work.

Brief History of Grading Systems

In 1785, students at Yale were ranked as "optimi", second optimi, inferiore, and pejores. At William and Mary, students were ranked No. 1 or No. 2, while Harvard used a 1-200 numerical system and, after 1883, "Classes" I to V, with V a failing grade. These examples show how inconsistent early grading was.

In 1887, Mount Holyoke College became the first college to use letter grades similar to today's: A, B, C, D, and E, where E meant failing (below 75%). The college later added F for failing. Letter grading then spread through colleges and high schools, though what counts as an A, or whether plus and minus grades are used, still varies by school.

An Alternative to Letter Grades

Letter grades summarise performance quickly and suit work with clear right or wrong answers, such as an algebra exam, but give less feedback on subjective work like a written paper. Some schools, such as Saint Ann's School in New York City, use written reports instead of letters to focus on learning rather than a grade. Most institutions still use letter grades, partly because written evaluations are hard to scale to courses with hundreds of students.
