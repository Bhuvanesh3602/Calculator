What Is Surface Area?

Surface area is the total area covering the outside of a three-dimensional object, measured in square units. It tells you how much paint, wrapping paper, fabric, insulation or coating an object needs. Each shape is described in more detail on the Volume Calculator and Area Calculator pages; this page focuses on the surface area equations and how to use them.

Worked Examples

Sphere

A sphere’s surface area depends only on its radius r:

SA = 4πr2

Example: a chocolate truffle with a radius of 0.325 inches has a surface area of 4 × π × 0.3252 = 1.327 in2, which is the area a coating would need to cover.

Cone

A closed cone has a circular base and a curved lateral surface that runs to the apex, where r is the base radius and h is the height:

base SA = πr2
lateral SA = πr√r2 + h2
total SA = πr(r + √r2 + h2)

Example: a conical rice hat with a radius of 0.4 ft and a height of 0.5 ft needs a lateral area of π × 0.4 × √0.42 + 0.52 = 0.805 ft2 of fabric. Adding the base gives a total of 1.307 ft2.

Cube

A cube has six identical square faces with edge length a:

SA = 6a2

Example: a custom puzzle cube with a 4 inch edge has a surface area of 6 × 42 = 96 in2, which is the area a printed or painted finish must cover.

Cylinder (Cylindrical Tank)

A closed cylinder adds two circular bases to its curved side, where r is the radius and h is the height:

bases SA = 2πr2
lateral SA = 2πrh
total SA = 2πr(r + h)

Example: a round tank with a radius of 3.5 ft and a height of 5.5 ft has a total surface area of 2π × 3.5 × (3.5 + 5.5) = 197.920 ft2. This number matters for heat loss and for how much paint or insulation you need.

Rectangular Tank (Box)

Add the areas of the three pairs of opposite faces, where l is the length, w is the width and h is the height:

SA = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh

Example: a gift box measuring 3 ft × 4 ft × 5 ft has a surface area of (2 × 3 × 4) + (2 × 4 × 5) + (2 × 3 × 5) = 94 ft2, so you need at least that much wrapping paper.

Capsule

A capsule is a cylinder capped by two hemispheres. The two hemispheres combine into one full sphere, and the flat bases of the cylinder are not part of the surface. Here r is the radius and h is the height of the cylindrical section:

SA = 4πr2 + 2πrh

Example: a pill with r = 0.05 in and h = 0.5 in has a surface area of 4π × 0.052 + 2π × 0.05 × 0.5 = 0.188 in2, which is useful for estimating coating quantities.

Spherical Cap

A spherical cap is the piece of a sphere cut off by a plane. This calculator assumes a solid cap and includes its flat base. For a hollow cap, such as a dome, use only the curved part. Here R is the sphere radius, r is the base radius and h is the cap height. These three values are linked by r2 = h(2R − h), so any two give the third:

curved SA = 2πRh
base SA = πr2
total solid SA = 2πRh + πr2

Example: a dome cut from a sphere with R = 0.80 ft and h = 0.53 ft has a curved surface of 2π × 0.80 × 0.53 = 2.664 ft2.

Conical Frustum

A conical frustum is a cone with its tip cut off. With top radius r, bottom radius R and height h:

ends SA = π(R2 + r2)
lateral SA = π(R + r)√(R-r)2 + h2
total SA = π(R2 + r2) + π(R + r)√(R-r)2 + h2

Example: a closed model volcano with R = 1 ft, r = 0.3 ft and h = 1.5 ft has a total surface area of π(12 + 0.32) + π(1 + 0.3)√(1 - 0.3)2 + 1.52 = 10.185 ft2.

Ellipsoid

No simple exact formula exists for the surface area of an ellipsoid. The calculator uses a well-known approximation, with p = 1.6, that is accurate for shapes not far from a sphere. Here a, b and c are the three semi-axes:

SA ≈ 4π · ((a1.6b1.6 + a1.6c1.6 + b1.6c1.6)/3)1/1.6

Example: an oval vegetable slice with axes of 0.1, 0.2 and 0.35 inches has an estimated surface area of about 0.56 in2.

Square Pyramid

A square pyramid has one square base and four triangular faces. With base edge a and height h:

base SA = a2
lateral SA = 2a√(a/2)2 + h2
total SA = a2 + 2a√(a/2)2 + h2

Example: a model pyramid with a = 3 ft and h = 5 ft has a total surface area of 32 + 2 × 3 × √(3/2)2 + 52 = 40.321 ft2.

Surface Area FAQ

How do you find the surface area of a shape?

Add up the area of every face or curved surface of the solid. Each shape has its own formula, such as 4πr² for a sphere or 6a² for a cube. Pick the shape above, enter the dimensions and click Calculate.

What is the difference between lateral and total surface area?

Lateral surface area counts only the sides, such as the curved wall of a cone or cylinder. Total surface area adds the flat or curved bases as well.

What units is surface area measured in?

Square units, such as ft², in², m² or cm². Use the same unit for every dimension, or choose units in each drop-down menu before calculating.

Is the ellipsoid result exact?

No. An ellipsoid has no simple exact formula, so the calculator uses an approximation that is very accurate for shapes close to a sphere.

Does the capsule formula include the flat ends?

A capsule has two hemispherical ends, so there are no flat bases. Its surface area is a full sphere (4πr²) plus the lateral area of the cylinder between them (2πrh).
